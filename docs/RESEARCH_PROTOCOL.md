# Research protocol

## Portfolio arithmetic

For each loaded security, `market value = whole shares × latest available daily close`. Net asset value is cash plus all market values. A security, sector, or trading-role weight is the relevant market value divided by NAV. Drift is current weight minus user-entered target weight, in percentage points. Target weights, including cash, must sum to 100%. Cost basis is shown as context and does not determine position sizing.

An add-review quantity is bounded by all of the following, using the latest model and close:

1. Remaining single-name capacity.
2. Remaining sector capacity.
3. Cash above the minimum reserve.
4. Planned risk budget divided by the planning stop distance.
5. Remaining security target value.

The lowest bound is converted to whole shares by rounding down. Tactical entries are additionally capped at one third of the remaining target gap. No return optimization or expected-return estimate is used. The policy fields are user assumptions. A plan cannot establish suitability, liquidity, or execution quality.

## Evidence and answer boundaries

- `M1` is the loaded daily-close series and its derived 20-day/50-day averages, 20-day return, 60-return annualized sample volatility, and maximum observed close-to-close drawdown.
- `P1` is the currently entered portfolio model and its calculated position/sector weights.
- `N1+` are recent articles with an explicit ticker tag and unique URL/headline. Their sentiment labels come from the provider. A named source count is not a test of editorial independence.
- `F1` is the provider overview snapshot. It has no point-in-time filing audit, intrinsic value estimate, or earnings forecast.

Answers are generated by deterministic rules for add, reduce, allocation, news, fundamentals, and observed risk questions. Unsupported questions receive an explicit evidence-limit response. The interface does not claim general conversational intelligence. Original article links and provider documentation are available in provider mode. Synthetic sample articles are labeled and are never linked as real evidence.

## Risk and control hierarchy

1. Validate US equity identity and data availability.
2. Recalculate NAV, target drift, cash reserve, single-name and sector exposure.
3. Apply role-specific add and reduction rules.
4. Generate only a review status and, where allowed, an in-memory paper draft.
5. Before any real trade outside TradeBo, independently verify issuer disclosures, current market data, account objectives, actual positions, taxes, fees, order type, and liquidity.

The five-calendar-day freshness threshold pauses all review actions when a provider close is stale or future-dated. The raw daily series is unadjusted; splits and dividends may invalidate comparisons across a corporate action. A planned stop distance is a sizing convention, not an assured fill or maximum loss.

## Design references

The following public projects informed **design choices**, rather than supplying copied portfolio algorithms:

| Project | Relevant idea | TradeBo decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [PyPortfolioOpt](https://github.com/PyPortfolio/PyPortfolioOpt) | Explicit objectives and constraints in portfolio construction | Make cash, target weights, and position/sector limits visible and editable. Expected-return optimization is not implemented because this prototype lacks robust estimates. |
| [QuantStats](https://github.com/ranaroussi/quantstats) | Transparent portfolio performance and risk reporting | Show descriptive volatility and drawdown with their observed-series limit. No performance report or attribution is claimed. |
| [vectorbt](https://github.com/polakowo/vectorbt) | Reproducible strategy testing | Reserve point-in-time backtesting, fees, and slippage for a later audited phase. No backtest claim appears in this release. |
| [OpenBB](https://github.com/openbb-finance/OpenBB) | Modular market-data access | Keep a server-side provider adapter and a visible sample/provider boundary. |

Allocation and rebalancing concepts follow the educational framing of [Investor.gov](https://www.investor.gov/additional-resources/general-resources/publications-research/info-sheets/beginners-guide-asset), while single-name and sector concentration are treated as explicit risks consistent with [FINRA's concentration-risk discussion](https://www.finra.org/investors/insights/concentration-risk). These sources do not endorse the default numeric limits in the sample model.

## Future validation gates

- Add adjusted price histories and corporate-action checks before relying on trend signals across splits or dividends.
- Audit article and issuer-filing provenance, deduplicate syndicated reporting, and measure relevance/sentiment errors.
- Add a point-in-time, out-of-sample backtest with realistic fees, slippage, delistings, and survivorship handling before discussing strategy performance.
- Support account imports only with clear privacy, reconciliation, and user approval controls.
- Evaluate any future language-model answer layer against source-grounded questions and refusal cases; preserve the deterministic rule result as the action authority.
